The countdown, decision and discard effects are FLAC encodings of the WAV files at
commit 9670029a2dae985cfff6de7f28db17276ab8c0c9, in this directory.

Encoding command, once per source:
  ffmpeg -i source.wav -map_metadata -1 -codec:a flac -compression_level 12 \
    -metadata_header_padding 0 output.flac

The countdown's floating-point samples become 24-bit PCM; the other effects retain
their 16-bit PCM samples. Sample rates and channel counts are unchanged.

Use FLAC for these short effects: a 64 kbps MP3 trial added about 25 ms of leading
decoder padding in WebKit. Chromium and WebKit decode the FLAC files with the
original sample counts and onset timing. Audio is still loaded eagerly.

The draw tick (draw-airy-tick.flac) is original and synthesized: one tick of white
noise through a 6.8 kHz band-pass (Q 1.1) and a 9 kHz low-pass, under a 1.2 ms
raised-cosine rise and 1.8 ms decay, faded out by 18 ms; 24 ms long, 16-bit mono
44.1 kHz, peak normalized to -1 dBFS and played at a volume that sets it about
14 dB under the discard clack.

The call voices (calls/*.mp3) are 32 kbps mono 24 kHz MP3s with their leading silence
cut losslessly, without re-encoding: the frames before the voice are dropped and
replaced by one silent frame (zero side info) whose main data carries the bit
reservoir the first kept frame reads, at the lowest MPEG-2 Layer III bitrate that
holds it. Each voice now starts 53-69 ms into its clip instead of 300-580 ms, and
decodes sample for sample as before from its onset in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox.
Keep the lead-ins even: a voice with a longer one is heard after a call played
just after it (a riichi discard dealt into a ron).
